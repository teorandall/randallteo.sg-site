What the three sums mean

Basic Retirement Sum

What CPF estimates covers basic living needs, assuming you own a property you can live in and pay no rent. You may set aside the BRS instead of the FRS if you pledge that property.

Full Retirement Sum

Exactly double the BRS. The default amount moved into your Retirement Account at 55, and the reference point for withdrawal rules. Savings above it can generally be withdrawn from 55.

Enhanced Retirement Sum

The ceiling you may voluntarily top up to for higher payouts. Since 2025 it has been four times the BRS. It is the one sum that does not lock to your cohort.

The distinction that catches most people out. Your BRS and FRS are fixed in the year you turn 55 and never change again. The ERS is not tied to your age or cohort at all — it rises every 1 January and applies to everyone aged 55 and above in that year. So if you turned 55 some years ago, your BRS and FRS come from your own row below, but your ERS is the one on this year's row.

Routes to close a shortfall

Property pledge

If you own a property, you can set aside the BRS rather than the FRS and withdraw the difference. No cash required. Payouts are correspondingly lower.

Cash top-up (RSTU)

Top up your RA with cash for tax relief of up to $8,000 a year for yourself, and another $8,000 for family members. Top up in January rather than December — CPF pays interest on the lowest balance in the year.

CPF transfer

Move OA savings to your RA to earn 4% instead of 2.5%. No tax relief, and the move cannot be reversed, so only transfer what you will not need for housing.
