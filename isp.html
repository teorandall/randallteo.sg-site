How it's calculated
  1. Pro-ration (Class A plans at a private hospital only): the plan only recognises a % of the bill — the rest is the client's.
  2. Deductible: the client pays this first. New riders cannot pay the S$3,500 minimum (MOH rules from 1 Apr 2026).
  3. Co-payment: with a rider the client pays 5% (Income: 5–10%) of the rest; without a rider, 10% co-insurance (GE P PRIME outside panel: 40%).
  4. Stop-loss: the rider caps the co-payment at S$6,000 a year — only with panel, pre-authorised extended panel (where offered) or restructured hospitals.
  5. Annual limit: anything above the plan's policy-year limit is the client's.

Every new rider works the same way at its core.

You pay the deductible (S$3,500 for most plans) plus a 5% co-payment. The stop-loss caps your co-payment at S$6,000 a year only with panel doctors, restructured hospitals or emergency care — non-panel treatment has no cap.

Biggest differences: panel rules and cancer drugs.

See table 2 Non-panel doctors and table 3 Cancer drugs NOT on MOH list — non-CDL cover ranges from S$5,000 to S$200,000+ a year. AIA, Raffles and Singlife need a paid cancer add-on for full cancer drug cover (Singlife's is the separate Cancer Cover Plus II plan).

Check the watch-outs.

GE P PRIME: S$5,000–6,000 deductible and 40% co-insurance outside partnering hospitals. PRU Preferred Care: no rider cover with non-panel doctors or at 'No Access' hospitals. Income Essential Care: 7% co-payment.

Restructured hospital plans · Class A ward

Main plans covering up to Class A wards in restructured (public) hospitals, with their new April 2026 riders.

Every new rider works the same way at its core.

You pay the deductible (S$3,500) plus a 5% co-payment. The stop-loss caps it at S$6,000 a year at panel providers, restructured hospitals or emergency — no cap otherwise.

Going private on a Class A plan is costly.

Bills are pro-rated — the plan pays only 35% to 70% of a private hospital bill depending on the insurer (table 1, Lower payout situations).

Cancer cover varies widely.

Non-CDL drug cover ranges from S$5,000 (Raffles Choice) to S$150,000/yr (PRU, GE). AIA Pro B builds cancer cover in. Raffles and Singlife need a paid cancer add-on for full cover (Raffles Cancer Guard; Singlife's separate Cancer Cover Plus II plan).

Where Prudential stands out — marked ★ in the tables

  • Pre/post-hospital 180 / 365 days with any doctor — others need panel / preferred providers for this length.
  • Deductible stays at S$3,500 until age 85 (others raise it from age 80–82), and Plus Care pays 95% of any deductible above S$3,500.
  • Planned overseas treatment covered on the main plan (also AIA & Singlife; GE, Income, Raffles don't).
  • High-cost drugs for rare conditions (haemophilia, SMA, thalassaemia, etc.) with no waiting period — HSBC has a 365-day wait; AIA only from 1 Oct 2026; others none.
  • Full cancer cover with no add-on — 20× MSHL for CDL drugs and S$150,000/yr for non-CDL drugs (Raffles and Singlife need a paid add-on).
  • Plus Care extras: extra S$50k limit for critical illness, TCM S$3,000/yr, accident outpatient S$2,000.

5Old riders (closed to new customers 1 Apr 2026)

Is it only the deductible and stop-loss? Mostly true for Class A.

Most old Class A riders pay the deductible and cap out-of-pocket at S$3,000 a year, vs S$9,500 on the new riders. Cancer cover is the same (AIA raised Max VitalHealth B to match Pro B in Oct 2025). How to read this
DeductibleThe amount you pay first each policy year before the plan pays.
Co-insurance / co-paymentYour % share of the bill after the deductible. Main plans: 10%. New riders bring it down to about 5%.
Panel / preferred providerSpecialists and hospitals approved by the insurer. Using them unlocks higher limits and the S$6,000 cap.
Pro-rationIf you go to a higher-class hospital than your plan covers, the plan pays only a % of the bill.
CDL · MSHLCDL = MOH's Cancer Drug List. MSHL = MediShield Life claim limit; '5×' means five times that limit.
CTGTCell, Tissue and Gene Therapy, e.g. CAR-T cancer treatments.
Stop-loss (co-payment cap)The most you pay in co-payment in a year. Only applies when you use panel doctors, restructured hospitals or emergency care.
